Therapists in Minneapolis–St. Paul

"Therapist" is a broad word that covers several licensed professions, including clinical social workers, marriage and family therapists, psychologists and counselors. All provide talk therapy; their training and focus differ.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or call 911.

What a therapist can help with

  • Anxiety, depression and trauma
  • Relationship and family issues
  • Life changes and stress
  • Understanding patterns in your thoughts and behavior

What to expect

Most therapists start with an intake session to understand your history and goals. Approaches vary, for example cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), EMDR or family systems therapy. Ask how they work.

Credentials to look for

Common Minnesota licenses include LICSW (Board of Social Work), LMFT (Board of Marriage and Family Therapy), LP (Board of Psychology) and LPCC (Board of Behavioral Health and Therapy). Each board has a public license lookup.

Questions to ask before you book

  • Which license do you hold?
  • What approach do you use, and why for my situation?
  • How long do people usually work with you?
  • What does a session cost, and do you take insurance?
